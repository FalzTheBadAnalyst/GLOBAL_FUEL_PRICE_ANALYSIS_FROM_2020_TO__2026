# GLOBAL_FUEL_PRICE_ANALYSIS_FROM_2020_TO__2026

**Research Questions**
How have petrol, diesel, and LPG prices changed between 2020 and 2026?

Which regions recorded the highest and lowest average fuel prices?

How do fuel prices differ across income levels within different regions?

How do fuel prices vary across subsidy levels within different regions?

What is the relationship between Brent crude oil prices and global fuel prices?

How does fuel taxation vary across regions, and how might it relate to petrol prices?

**Project Summary**
I conducted a Global Fuel Price Analysis covering 84 countries across 7 geographical regions from 2020 to 2026. I used Excel and SQL to clean, organize, analyze, and validate the dataset, focusing on global fuel price trends, regional price differences, income levels, subsidy policies, taxation, and Brent crude oil prices. After completing the analysis, I used Power BI to transform the findings into an interactive dashboard, presenting key insights through KPIs, tables, and visualizations. The dashboard provides an overview of global fuel price movements and highlights differences in fuel prices across regions, income categories, and subsidy levels.

**Tools Used** 
Excel | SQL | Power BI

**Key Insights from the Global Fuel Price Dashboard**

Global Coverage: The analysis covered 84 countries across 7 geographical regions, with average fuel prices of $2.28 for petrol, $2.13 for diesel, and $1.71 for LPG.

Yearly Fuel Price Trends: Fuel prices generally increased between 2020 and 2026, although all three fuel types experienced a decline in 2023. Petrol increased from $1.48 in 2020 to $2.65 in 2026, diesel increased from $1.39 to $2.47, while LPG increased from $1.11 to $1.99.

Brent Crude Oil Relationship: Brent crude oil prices increased from $69.44 in 2020 to $123.75 in 2026, following a similar general upward movement to petrol, diesel, and LPG prices. Both crude oil and fuel prices recorded a decline in 2023 before increasing again.

Regional Fuel Prices: Europe recorded the highest average petrol price at $3.70, followed by Oceania at $3.66, while the Middle East recorded the lowest at $1.23. The same regional pattern was observed for diesel and LPG, with Europe recording the highest average prices of $3.52 and $2.78 respectively, while the Middle East recorded the lowest at $1.14 and $0.92.

Income Level Analysis: Fuel prices varied across income categories within different regions. Europe and Oceania recorded relatively high fuel prices across several income categories, while some other regions recorded lower prices. This suggests that income classification alone does not explain regional fuel price differences.

Subsidy Level Analysis: Fuel prices also varied across subsidy categories within different regions. The differences suggest that government subsidy policies may influence the prices consumers pay for fuel, although other market conditions and government policies may also contribute to the variations.

Fuel Taxation: Oceania recorded the highest average tax percentage at 38.69%, followed by Europe at 34.61%, while South America recorded the lowest at 18.20%. The findings highlight differences in fuel taxation policies across regions, which may contribute to variations in consumer fuel prices.
